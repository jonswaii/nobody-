I.
Manifesto

Before it was a sport, it was a ritual. Before it was content, it was a camp: two-a-days, cracked shins, a teacher who never said good job. We make clothes for that.

We don't dress fans. We dress people who show up: the 6 AM runner, the kid on the bag in a garage, the coach who's held pads for thirty years and never been on a poster.

Every piece is built like fight gear and cut like fashion. Real leather, real weight, small runs. Nothing loud for the sake of it. The work is the loud part.

The Gallery

III.
The Culture

The art of eight limbs.

Fists, elbows, knees, shins. Muay Thai is where we start, because it never forgot where it came from. Before the first strike there's respect, music and a prayer. These are the pieces we build around.

ไหว้ครูรำมวย

Wai Kru Ram Muay

The dance before the fight. A bow to your teacher, your camp and the ring itself. Every fighter's is a little different, passed down from the gym that raised them.

มงคล

Mongkhon

The sacred headpiece, blessed and placed by the teacher. Worn through the ritual, taken off by the corner right before the first bell. Never touched by just anybody.

ประเจียด

Pra Jiad

The armbands. Protection, luck, a piece of home tied around the bicep. Some carry a scrap of cloth from a mother's sarong.

สะระหม่า

Sarama

The live music. Pipe, drums and cymbals that start slow with the ritual and speed up with the fight. The ring has a heartbeat, and you can hear it.

Respect the teacher. Respect the opponent. Then go to work.
House rule, No. 1
